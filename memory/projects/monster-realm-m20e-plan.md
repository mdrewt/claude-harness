## Contract rulings (test-design binding)

- **AM4 (red-team 2 — golden fixture is TWO-LAYER).** `fixtures/breadcrumb-golden.json` schema per case: `{ id, evt, extra_fields_json, breadcrumb: {cause, sched, phase}, expected_module_json, host_line }`. Rust mirror asserts `build_log_line(...) == expected_module_json` byte-for-byte. JS `parseHostLine` consumes `host_line` (the host-wrapped envelope whose `message` embeds `expected_module_json`) and the parse test asserts the extracted payload equals `expected_module_json` plus the outer `ts`/`function` fields. Neither consumer ever tests the other's layer. `host_line` shape seeded from `config.alloy:31-40` + the m20d plan APPENDIX; **confirmed against one live captured line during T8 (mandatory runbook step; mismatch = fix fixture pre-PR)**.
- **AM5 (red-team 1 — G9f granularity).** "Paired reducer" = enter AND exit literals **within one reducer function's body**, via brace-tracking function-body extraction (mirror `observability_tests.rs`'s `fn_body` idiom). The eval **exports** its scanner (`pairedReducers(srcMap)`); tooth T-c calls **that exact exported function**, plus a **negative control**: enter in reducer A + exit in reducer B (same file) must NOT register. The Rust mirror scan (T5) implements the same function-body rule independently.
- **AM6 (red-team 3 — ts ordering).** All ts comparisons in `pair.mjs` are **BigInt** comparisons of the digit strings. Dedicated tooth: mixed-digit-length ts values where lexicographic and numeric order disagree (e.g. "999" vs "1000000000000000"). G8 fixture includes at least one mixed-length case.
- **AM7 (red-team 5 — seeded ambiguity is the brief's own G8 requirement).** G8 fixture has BOTH: (a) two interleaved chains with distinct keys → no cross-pollination; (b) a **reused key across two causally-unrelated chains** (same cause value, and separately same `sched.target_reducer+scheduled_at`) → pinned FIFO-by-ts tie-break, documented as the chosen (imperfect, D15-disclosed) behavior. The inline deliberately-broken LIFO pairer must fail (a).
- **AM8 (red-team 10 — duplicate-evt detection).** Two-stage: outer `JSON.parse` of the raw line; then a raw character-walk of the still-unparsed `message` STRING value (brace/bracket-depth + in-string tracker, no regex) detecting a duplicate top-level `"evt"` key before the inner parse. Counter-teeth: escaped lookalike in the outer raw line → no fire; nested non-top-level `evt` → no fire; true top-level duplicate → fire.
- **AM9 (red-team 12 — unpaired ⇒ no span).** Chosen interpretation recorded in the ADR amendment: unpaired breadcrumbs are diagnostic-only (counted, listed), never spans — deliberately stronger than D15's illustrative "we know this happened" wording.
- **AM10 (red-team 4 — G5 ambient-traffic collision).** The synthetic G5 fixture lines use a **run-unique synthetic `function` value** (`mr_m20e_g5_<runid>`) so the delta series cannot collide with real `mr_heartbeat` traffic; the live half asserts the series was absent at V0 (or V0 recorded per-series) and reaches ≥ N on the SYNTHETIC series only. **Note: `evt` label value stays `"heartbeat"`** (bounded enum C8) — uniqueness comes from the reducer/function label.
- **AM11 (red-team 9 — $slo_set double-copy).** G9e parses **both** matcher occurrences (`recording.rules.yml:29` and `:32`), asserts the two parsed sets are identical, and fails loud on divergence or on <1 name from either.
- **AM12 (red-team 7 — G9j ban list extended).** In `relay/**`: ban `new RegExp(`, `node:child_process`, `node:http`, `node:net`, `.listen(`, `setInterval(`, `setTimeout(`; ban `Date.now(` in the four pure files (batch CLI may not use it either — nothing in relay/** needs a clock). This mechanically prevents the parked `/health`/daemon from creeping back in-slice.
- **AM13 (red-team 8 — floors not hand-picked).** Golden fixture: required **categories** enumerated in the test (escaping, duplicate-key forgery, i64 `content_version` bounds, non-JSON line, mixed-length ts, cause-keyed + sched-keyed cases) — each category asserted present by id, not a bare count.
