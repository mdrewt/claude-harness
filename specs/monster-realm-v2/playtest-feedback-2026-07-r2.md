## Verdict (fun gate)

## What played well
- Movement is improved since the last playtest, it is much easier to walk to an intended location, but is still a little glitchy.
- I completed a full session end to end: wild encounters, a successful recruit, multiple battles, fusion menu, inventory, trading (two browser windows), shopping, party management.

## What didn't
- When my primary monster has 0 health and a battle starts, the primary monster is the one sent out in the first round. If I click on one of the attack buttons, it looks like the attack is processed (even though the monster has 0hp), and the next round starts with my second monster instead.
- UI/UX friction dominated the session: inconsistent overlay design (fusion, inventory, and box uses an overlay; trade, shop, talk, and quest are unreadable bottom-of-page panels). UI/UX needs a complete overhaul.
- I walked up to the one random NPC on the may and pressed "T" but the menu has the same issues I complained about before. It opens at the bottom of the page, changes the page size, and I have to scroll down to see it. This is different from how dialogue works in other games.
- Non-responsive viewport. The game's map remains the same size regardless of the browser's window size. In web design there's a concept called "responsive design" where elements on the page automatically resize to fit the size of the viewport. I believe that a similar technique should be used here. Additionaly everything should fit within the current size of the viewport (the app should not change the page size unless the browser window resizes and it should never require the player to scroll down the page. In-menu scrolling is different from page scrolling and can be allowed. New menus/pages/elements can be rendered on top of the existong view or replace it as appropriate).
- Movement issues are still present. I would walk all the way to the left edge, pause, then tap right and somtimes the character will travel a single tile to the right, sometimes they will travel two tiles. It appears inconsistantly.
- `?` help overlay requires the player to press `shift` + `/` instead of just `/`. Requiring a modifier key is unexpected behavior.
- There is currently only a single fusion/evolution recipe, which may be fine for right now but that list will need to be expanded once the project is feature complete and we begin adding playable content in the future. I like that there is a list of potential recipes. I do wonder why the instructions for the menu specify both fusion and evolution. We should decide on one cohesive system rather than implementing multiple different ones.
- The map is very simplistic and small with a very limited tileset and the overall design isn't very visually appealing.
- Having no default "action"/"interact" key is awkward. In most other games, there are a few standard buttons (the gamebow advance only had A, B, LB, RB, start, select, and the d-pad) and their behavior is contextual. So, rather than haveing a single key for every single possible action, each button could potentially do different things. For example, and "interact" button would deterministically decide its behavior by checking what is in front of the character. An NPC could trigger the "talk", "trade", or "challenge" commands (either by having a default action fron NPCs, or by allowing the player to select an action from a menu), while a vending machine (or a shopkeeper NPC) could trigger a "shop" command that opens up that shop's menu, and an empty space could trigger a noop (no operation). Walking around and interacting with things promotes exploration and is more fun than memorizing the correct key bindings and being able to shop/trade/challenge from anywhere on the map. it is also a good way to hide easter eggs for the player to find.
- I don't like the fusion mechanic (as it currently exists).
- There's not a lot of content at the moment (Only 1 map/area that is small in size, limited interactability, little to do other than the main gameplay loop of fighting and recruiting monsters, very little story/progression).

- I previously suggested a change to the fusion/evolution system that that had many of its key aspects changed, removed, or watered-down. I am overruling those previous decisions. I want a redesign of the evolusion/fusion system. Fusing multiple monsters into a new monster is no longer a feature. Instead, monsters have evolution trees (similar to the old fusion recipies) and will evolve by aquiring different types of "essence". I call it a evolution "tree", but the actual data structure should be closer to a graph (monsters as nodes, evolution paths as edges, direction is one-way) with there multiple paths to evolve into a specific monster from different starting monsters and different paths for a specific starting monster to evolve into. Each path will have its own requirements and evolution will not occur until one of a monster's possible paths are met. The path a monster takes down its evolution tree can each depend on several factors (some visible, some invisible) such as the following:
  - The monster's history. How well it was raised/cared for using stats like "Quality Time" (amount of playtime with the monster actively in the party), "Trust" (Ratio of favorable actions vs unfavorable actions, pre-seeded with k=10 to prevent it from changing too quickly due to a small sample size), and "Nutrition" (A measure of how close to the maximum the monster's EVs are, since the primary method for raising EVs will be permanently consuming rare food items). These stats are only suggestions/examples at this time.
  - The monster's tier/stage. Similar to the Digimon games each monster will have a standardized tier that determines how high up on their evolution tree they are. Monsters will move up their evolution tree to a higher tier by evolving and any eggs they have will start at the lowest tier. Monsters shouldn't "downgrade" to a lower tier.
  - The monster's level. Higher tier evolutions have higher level requirements. Additionally, the level required by different evolutions within the same tier can also vary with "rarer" evolution options having higher requirements.
  - The quantity of aquired essence types. Each path on the evolution tree should require specific amounts of different essence types. For example, if lightning is considered a combination of fire and air, then a tier 1 fire-type monster might require 100 air essence to evolve into a tier 2 lightning-type monster, while an air-type monster might require 100 fire essence, and a normal-type monster might  require both 75 fire essence and 75 air essence. Monsters should be able to aquire essence from defeating other monsters in battle, performing specific types of training (at a much slower aquisition rate), or by being feed "crystalized essence" (a rare item). Higher tier (or "rarer") evolutions with have higher essence requirements than lower tier (or more "common") evolutions. In some ways, "essence" is a little similar to "EVs" in pokemon, except essence doesn't directly affect stats and the amounts of essence a monster has aquired is reset to 0 after each evolution (the essence gets used up).
